why

Why do I
need help with everything?

I dance.
I sing.
And still, I don't know how to do
a damn thing.

I thought I changed.

Really,
I haven't.

I inflate myself—
a balloon stretched thin,
waiting for the sound
of its own collapse.

And when it bursts,
nobody minds.

I could die tomorrow
and the earth would be lighter,

a weight lifted
from its own shoulders,

and my head
from mine.

I don't talk.

I'm afraid of what other people think
when they look at me.

I don't do enough.

Everything I am
exists inside
a false sense of identity.

I act intelligent.
I pretend I know so much.

Then someone asks me to prove it
and I reach for something else,
anything else,

something that can speak
for me.

I'm dirty on the inside.

The grime—

it eats away
at my consciousness.

I don't know how to think
when I place a question
in front of myself.

A question of what?

I don't know.

It could be anything:

a song,
a creation,
a work of art.

Nothing escapes
the tips of my fingers,
my tongue,
my eyes,
my teeth.

I sit there
and surrender
before I've even begun.

And when I finally try—

when I think
I've found something—

I stop.

I don't know why.

My jaw hangs loose.

A twelve-gauge Remington
chambered with buckshot—

the thought tears through my skull,
through gray matter,
through everything I thought
I was.

It doesn't stop
until it's finished.

It's all so unfair.

Why couldn't it have been
another time?

Another place?

A different beginning?

I could learn
from the ground up.

But I'm afraid
of losing comfort.

Afraid of anything
that requires

a steady back,
tough skin,
calloused hands,
broad shoulders.

Maybe that's why
everyone leaves.

Maybe I was never enough.

My old man—

I think he hates me too.

He could watch me hurt.
Watch me leave.

And I don't think
he'd feel a thing.

He isn't proud
of what I've become.

He's disappointed
in what I haven't.

My friends—

they leave eventually.

They grow tired
of my perpetual existence,

my constant presence,

the noise I make
just by being here.

A symphony
I play for every ear,

and nobody
stays long enough
to listen.

My fingers
toiled at the one thing
I thought I was good at

until today.

Until every day.
